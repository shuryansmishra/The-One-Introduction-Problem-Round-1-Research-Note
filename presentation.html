Problem Formulation Sequential Reciprocal Matching

The Real-World Reciprocal Matching Bottleneck

Matching adult participants over a 60-day horizon differs fundamentally from classical one-sided recommendation. Both sides must mutually consent through an extended, multi-stage stochastic funnel.

1. Multi-Stage Funnel

Introduction $\rightarrow$ Mutual Acceptance (7-day window) $\rightarrow$ First Date (up to 30 days) $\rightarrow$ Mutual Second-Meeting Intention (MSMI, within 3 days).

2. Delayed & Censored

Dates take up to 30 days to happen. Introductions on Day 45 remain unresolved on Day 60. Naive algorithms label pending introductions as negative, destroying true compatibility signals.

3. Combinatorial Blocking

Candidates cannot be assigned more than once per day. Greedy edge ranking causes attractive candidates to block globally optimal networks, starving competing members.

Theoretical Traps Why Standard Heuristics Fail

Four Structural Traps in Sequential Matching

Trap 1: Greedy Starvation & Blocking

Pairing popular candidate $B$ with suitor $A$ (score 0.95) blocks $(A, D)$ (0.65) and $(B, C)$ (0.65), producing total utility 0.95 instead of the globally optimal 1.30.

Trap 2: Delayed-Feedback Bias

Pending introductions in progress are treated as rejections by static learners, causing severe negative weight decay on features that are actually succeeding.

Trap 3: Selective-Label Feedback Loops

Outcomes are only observed for introduced pairs. Without Bayesian exploration, the algorithm reinforces early biases and ignores high-potential unexplored archetypes.

Trap 4: Distribution Drift & Shifts

In `drift` and `shift` scenarios, feature importance changes abruptly mid-stream. Static policies lock into stale weights and collapse in the second half of the horizon.

System Architecture End-to-End Decision Pipeline

Unified Daily Decision Pipeline

On each simulation day, our policy executes a sequential 6-stage offline pipeline:

[Stage 1: State Ingestion] ──> Ingest public observation & state dictionary (strictly offline)
[Stage 2: Kaplan-Meier Labeling] ──> Assign soft labels $\tilde{y}(t) \in [0.05, 0.40]$ to pending dates
[Stage 3: Laplace Posterior Update] ──> Update Gaussian beliefs with discount $\gamma = 0.98$
[Stage 4: Thompson Weight Sampling] ──> Draw stochastic feature weights $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_t, \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_t)$
[Stage 5: Cost-Normalized VOI Asks] ──> Greedily spend 12 units on highest $\text{VOI}(q)/\text{Cost}(q)$ queries
[Stage 6: Edmonds' Blossom Match] ──> Maximize cardinality first with base weight $1000 + U_{ij}$

Innovation 1 Survival-Weighted Delay Correction

Kaplan-Meier Non-Parametric Delay Correction

Let $T$ denote the duration from introduction to terminal outcome. For an introduction still unresolved after elapsed time $t$, we compute its conditional probability of eventual success:

$$\tilde{y}(t) = \frac{\pi [1 - F_1(t)]}{\pi [1 - F_1(t)] + (1 - \pi)}$$

Pending introductions receive soft labels $\tilde{y}(t) \in [0.05, 0.40]$. Feature weights are protected from premature degradation, enabling robust learning under right-censored delay.

Decisive Delayed Scenario Advantage

0.50 MSMI / 100 in Delayed Dates Scenario

Triples the score of delay-unaware models (Ablation 2: 0.17 MSMI). Preserves matching quality when date outcomes arrive up to 30 days after initial introduction.

Innovation 2 Combinatorial Graph Optimization

Edmonds' Blossom Global Allocation vs Greedy

We formulate daily pairing as a Maximum-Weight General Graph Matching Problem over the feasible reciprocal candidate graph:

$$\max_{\boldsymbol{x} \in \{0, 1\}^{|\mathcal{E}|}} \sum_{\{i, j\} \in \mathcal{E}} (1000 + U_{ij}) x_{ij} \quad \text{s.t.} \sum_{j} x_{ij} \le 1$$

The base weight of 1000 guarantees maximum cardinality (matching every possible available pair), while Thompson-sampled utilities $U_{ij}$ break ties globally.

Ablation Confirmation (H1)

+125% MSMI Gain over Greedy Matching

Removing Edmonds' Blossom matching (Ablation 1) causes MSMI to collapse from 0.250 down to 0.111 (-56%), proving that global edge resolution is mandatory.

Innovation 3 Decision-Theoretic Inquiry

Cost-Normalized Value of Information (VOI)

1. ROI-Ranked Inquiry

Instead of uniform asking, queries are ranked by expected utility delta per budget unit: $\text{Priority}(q) = \text{VOI}(q)/\text{Cost}(q)$.

2. Arrival Horizon Boost

New arrivals (last 3 days) receive a 1.4× ROI priority boost for hard bundles: they have the longest remaining active horizon and unlock fresh edges.

3. Soft Query Threshold

Soft preference queries (cost 1) are restricted to members with $\ge 2$ feasible candidates, ensuring questions only spend budget where answers break ties.
